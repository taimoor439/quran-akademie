How long should a Quran lesson be for a child?

A child's Quran lesson should match their age: about 25 minutes for ages 5–7, 30 minutes for ages 8–10, and 30–45 minutes from age 11. Short, regular lessons beat long ones — a focused 25 minutes, four or five times a week, builds reading skill faster than one long weekend session.

What will my child learn, step by step?

Your child starts with the Noorani Qaida — the Arabic letters and how they join — which takes 3–6 months. Then comes reading fluency with Tajweed, usually within 12–18 months, and after that Hifz (memorisation) or Tafseer (understanding), depending on your family's goals.

  1. 1

    Noorani Qaida — first letters

    3–6 months

    Your child learns the 28 Arabic letters, how they join together, and the vowel marks (harakaat) that make words readable. By the end of the Qaida, your child can read simple Arabic words on their own.

    Noorani Qaida course →
  2. 2

    Reading fluency with Tajweed

    12–18 months in total

    Now your child reads from the Mushaf itself — short surahs first, then longer passages — while the tutor corrects pronunciation letter by letter. This is where Tajweed becomes second nature.

    Tajweed course →
  3. 3

    Hifz — memorising the Quran

    3–6 years

    Children who want to memorise start with Juz Amma, the 30th juz, and build up surah by surah with a daily revision system (sabaq, sabqi, manzil). Hifz is a long journey — and children who start young finish strong.

    Hifz course →
  4. 4

    Understanding — Tafseer & Islamic studies

    Ongoing

    Reading is only half the journey. In Tafseer, children learn what the verses mean; in Islamic studies they learn aqeedah, daily duas and good manners (akhlaq) — the knowledge that turns reading into understanding.

    Tafseer course →

How will I know what my child is learning?

Every month you receive a written progress report showing exactly what your child has learned — which surahs and pages, how their Tajweed is developing, and what comes next. On the Standard plan, the report comes with a parent check-in with the tutor.

The report is simple and specific: which surahs and pages your child covered, which letters or rules still need work, and what the tutor plans next. No vague grades — you can see the actual progress, week by week.

Between reports, you are always welcome to sit near the lesson and listen. Parents who hear a lesson now and then know exactly where their child stands — and children try a little harder when Mum or Dad is nearby.

What happens in a children's Quran lesson?

Each lesson follows the same friendly rhythm: a quick revision of last week's verses, new material taught slowly with the tutor listening and correcting, reading practice, and a short dua or story to finish. Lessons are short, upbeat and full of praise — children look forward to them.

1

Warm-up & revision

The lesson opens with a quick revision of last week's verses — children love showing what they remember, and revision is how memorisation sticks.

2

New lesson

The tutor introduces the new letters or verses slowly, reading first so your child can listen, then reading together, then letting your child try alone.

3

Reading practice

Your child reads back while the tutor listens and corrects — gently, patiently, and always with praise for what is right.

4

A happy ending

Every lesson closes with a short dua or a story, and a small piece of homework for the week — usually just a few minutes of practice a day.

Frequently asked questions

What age should a child start learning Quran?

Most children start between the ages of 5 and 7, once they can sit and focus for a short lesson. Some start at 4 with very short sessions; others start later and catch up quickly. The free trial class is the best way to find out if your child is ready.

Read the full guide →

How long does it take a child to learn to read the Quran?

Most children starting from scratch reach basic reading fluency in 12–18 months, with 4–5 lessons a week and a few minutes of daily practice. The Noorani Qaida itself takes 3–6 months. Every child is different — the tutor adjusts the pace to your child, not the other way round.

See the full timeline →

How many Quran classes per week should a child take?

Four to five short lessons a week works best for most children — it builds a daily habit without tiring them out. The Basic plan has 4 classes a week and the Standard plan has 5; both are designed around how children actually learn.

Compare plans →

What if my child misses a class?

Life happens — let your tutor know in advance and the lesson can be rescheduled. The Standard plan includes flexible rescheduling, so a missed week never means a lost week.

Do parents need to be present during lessons?

Yes — a parent or guardian must stay nearby and be present in the home for the whole lesson. It is part of our safeguarding policy, and it also helps: children focus better when a parent is close by.
